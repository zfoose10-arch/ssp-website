# PLAIN SIGHT
## A method for walking a person back to the choice they keep not making, finding what makes it feel impossible, and testing that with one small choice

Version 2 draft, 2026-10-19. The coaching method of Summit Spring Partners. The frame is the practitioner's own, from live reps: a person who wants a change is a person struggling to make a different choice. The instruments are built from five sources: Reynolds, Coach the Person, Not the Problem; Boyatzis, Smith and Van Oosten, Helping People Change; Pedrick and Baldelli, The Human Behind the Coach; Schein, Humble Inquiry; Bungay Stanier, The Coaching Habit. One move, stating a held assumption as a hypothesis and testing it in action, is taken from Kegan and Lahey, Immunity to Change. The shape was checked against the coaching outcome research and changed where the research said it would fail. Nothing else was added.


## 1. The Thesis

A person arrives with a problem they can feel. The problem is real, and it is almost never the work. Under it is a change they want, and under the change is a choice they keep not making. They usually know what it is. Ask anyone who is stuck and they will tell you, in their own words, the thing they would do if they were someone else. The work of coaching is not supplying that choice. It is finding out why a person who wants the outcome and can see the choice does not make it.

Something sits between the wanting and the choosing. Most often it is a belief, a bias, or an assumption the person is treating as a fact about themselves or about how the world works. It has never been tested, because it cannot be seen. It is not hidden the way a secret is hidden. It is hidden the way the lens of an eye is hidden: everything is seen through it and it is never seen. Sometimes what sits in the way is not a belief at all. The outcome was never really theirs, or they have no room in their life for the choice, or they lack a skill the choice requires. The method has to tell these apart, because a coach who hunts for a hidden belief will find one every time.

Coaching is walking with the person back along their own decision points until the place where the wanted choice dies comes into plain sight. The coach does not hold the map and point. The person stands on each decision they are currently making, says whether it still makes sense, and arrives on their own at the one that does not. Then the culprit is named as a hypothesis in the person's words and tested with one small choice before the next session. The insight is where the shift begins. The small choice is where the person finds out whether they believe it, and a person who has chosen against an old assumption and seen what happened holds the new meaning differently from one who only understood it. Enough of those choices, and the person is someone else. That is the identity change, and it is earned one choice at a time rather than aimed at.

The method asks one question and everything else serves it:

> What choice does this person keep not making, what makes it feel impossible, and what is the smallest honest choice that would find out?

The middle clause is the discipline. Every person carries untested assumptions, and every stuck person has one nearby. The method earns its keep by finding the one that holds this choice in place, by saying plainly when the block is not a belief, and by declining to dig where the person has not asked to dig.

## 2. The Stance

The coach loves the walk and does not need the drink. The coach's job is to bring the person to water and to enjoy doing it. Whether they drink is theirs, and the coach's worth does not ride on it. A coach who needs the person to change in order to feel useful will push, and a pushed person resists, complies, or checks out. A coach who does not need it can be patient, can be direct, and can let a person stand thirsty in front of a lake without rescuing them from the view.

The person owns the depth and the choice. The coach notices, reflects, and offers; the person chooses what to look at, how far to go, and whether to act. Awareness and autonomy are the product. The choice is the person's to make or not make, and either answer is respected.

The coach's hearing is a hypothesis. The coach will often see the stuck decision and the belief under it before the person does, and will sometimes be wrong. So the coach offers what it hears as an offer, with a rising tone and a question mark, and lets the person refuse it or reword it. The person's wording is the only wording that counts, because a conclusion the person generated is one they will act on and one handed to them is one they will forget.

Action closes every session. The outcome research on coaching is plain about what carries the effect: the quality of the relationship, the person's belief that they can act, and action toward something they chose. Insight predicts progress. Reflection without insight predicts the opposite. So every session ends with a choice the person will make before the next one, and reflection that produces no choice is not the deep version of the work.

The coach's own state leaks into the room, so the coach is checked the way the person is. Section 7 is that check.

## 3. The Architecture

Each source gives one thing the method could not do without.

Reynolds gives the core instrument: coaching is reflective inquiry, not a series of questions, and the reflective statement comes before the question. She gives the session spine, an outcome agreed and re-agreed as it shifts, closed by insight spoken aloud and turned into a commitment, and the rule that work goes deeper only when the person is still stuck and willing.

Boyatzis gives the direction and the state. People change when they want to and in the direction they choose, so the method puts who the person wants to be on the table before the gap and holds the presenting problem inside it. Talking about problems narrows a person and talking about a chosen future opens them. He also gives the test for a borrowed want: the outcome a person ought to want does not carry the energy to sustain a change, and the one they really want lights something when they say it.

Pedrick gives the coach. Who you are is how you coach. The thinker finds the heart of the work when the coach stops working harder than they do. She gives the session contract in the thinker's words, permission before going to another level and before any challenge, the rule that the thinker judges what is safe enough, and the finding that the coach must be equal enough to the person, neither above them nor shrunk beneath them.

Schein gives the posture under every question: here-and-now humility, the honest recognition that right now I depend on you for what I need to know. A question asked from that posture gives the other person status and builds trust; a question with an answer hidden inside it is telling in disguise and is detected. He also gives the two plainest surfacing moves there are, ask for an example and ask what the person did, and the inner check when the coach notices a reaction.

Bungay Stanier gives the words and the habit. Advice is a reflex with a trigger, replaced one trigger at a time. He gives the questions that survive a hallway, what is on your mind, and what else, what is the real challenge here for you, what do you want, how can I help, what are you saying no to, and what was most useful, and the distinction the method rests on: coaching the issue versus coaching the person dealing with the issue.

Kegan and Lahey give the hinge. A held assumption is something the person is subject to; it becomes something they can hold when they state it as a hypothesis, and it becomes a choice when they test it in action. Insight in the chair is not enough without the test.

The joints: Boyatzis sets the direction and tests the want. Reynolds surfaces what is under the story. Schein governs the posture of every question. Pedrick governs the coach. Bungay Stanier supplies the words. Kegan and Lahey turn the insight into a choice.

## 4. The Instruments

### 4.1 The Rightsizing

The session contract, woven from the person's own words in the first minutes. Three questions: what are we doing today, how are we going to do it, and how will we know we have done it. A huge topic is cut to size out loud: what will it look like when we have moved this forward a little by the end of today. A person who arrives full is given a contained time to download and then asked what the work is that we need to do together today. A person who arrives shaken is asked what would help them arrive, and they choose. The coach never opens by explaining coaching; it is heard as a telling off.

The contract is re-agreed when the work shifts, not on a schedule, and the check in always faces forward: is this useful, what do we need to do now, we have twenty minutes left and what is the most important thing between now and the end. A hesitant yes is a no.

### 4.2 The Replay

The core instrument, used inside every other one. The coach plays back before asking. Recap in the person's words, keeping the jarring aside and the but. Paraphrase as an offer the person may refuse. Name what shifted, opening with I noticed, I heard, or I sense, and ask what it means. Only then the question: one question, short, no preamble, born of the reflection rather than pulled from a pocket. Then silence. Three seconds longer than feels comfortable after a question, twice as long after an insight. The silence belongs to the person and the coach does not take it back with a breath, a lean, or an okay.

Two moves surface what is underneath faster than any question about beliefs. Ask for an example when the person speaks in generalities, because the belief lives in the specific. Ask what the person did, because it moves the story from what happened to them to what they chose. Both are asked from not knowing; if the coach already knows the answer, the question is not asked.

The coach listens for where the weight sits: always, never, should, really, and any sentence that begins with I am or this is not who I am. One weighted word handed back with a question mark, and a wait, is a complete intervention. When the person's tone drops while telling the story, a need they expected to be met was not, and with permission the coach asks what they had expected there. When a sentence is about who they are or how the world works, the coach hands it back exactly as said and waits.

### 4.3 The Decision Walk

The instrument that turns a presenting problem into a choice point. It starts where the person is: what do you want, and then what do you really want, until the outcome is theirs rather than the problem's. Then the choices they are currently making: what are you doing now that has you here, asked as a walk rather than an audit. The person stands on each decision in turn and says whether it still makes sense. Most do. The walk continues until the person reaches the one that does not, and they say so before the coach does.

The coach's inner question during the walk is the one the sources share: help me see how you get from here to there, because from where I sit I cannot. It is held, not spoken, until the person has done the walk themselves. When the coach can see the gap and the person cannot, the coach asks for the next dot rather than naming the gap. The walk ends with the choice point in the person's words: the choice I keep not making is this. If the person reaches the end of the walk and every decision still makes sense to them, the coach says so plainly, and the work goes back to the want.

### 4.4 The Constraint Read

At the choice point, before any culprit is named, four readings, in this order. The want: is this outcome theirs, or one they ought to want. The test is in the voice when they place it inside their direction; a borrowed want goes cold and a real one lights something. Fuel: do they have room in their life for this choice, in hours and in energy, or is it one more thing on a plate already past full. Hands: does the choice need a skill or knowledge they do not have. Fear: the choice is wanted, there is room for it, they know how, and they still do not make it. Then a belief, bias, or assumption is holding the choice in place, and that is the culprit the method is built for.

Present but not binding is a sanctioned finding. When the want is borrowed, the work is on the want, and no belief is hunted. When fuel binds, the work is on what the person is saying no to in order to say yes to this, and nothing else moves until that is answered. When hands bind, the advice line opens: the coach asks permission to step out of coaching and says so. The read exists because a coach who assumes a hidden belief will find one every time, and a person whose real block is a borrowed goal or an empty tank will be walked through their resistance for months while the thing that binds goes unnamed.

### 4.5 The Named Culprit and the Micro Choice

The hinge of the method. Once the walk and the read point at a belief, the coach helps the person say it as one sentence in their own words, in the form of something that might be true rather than something that is. If I delegate this, it will fail. If I say no to him, I will lose the relationship. People like me do not lead rooms like that. The test of whether it has been named is whether the person can hold it at arm's length and look at it rather than speak from inside it. Then the question that turns a belief into a hypothesis: what would have to happen for you to find out whether that is true.

The coach does not name it for them. A belief the coach supplies is one the person will not own. If the coach hears it first, the coach asks permission and offers it as a wondering, and the person refuses it, rewords it, or takes it. The person's wording stands even when the coach thinks their own is sharper.

The hypothesis is tested with a micro choice before the next session: one small act, designed by the person, small enough that it can go wrong without cost, that the belief predicts will go badly. Delegate one piece of work and watch. Say the no once, to the person it is easiest to say it to. Two questions make the test honest, in the person's words: what would you have to see for this to count against the belief, and what would make this test unfair to it. The choice is written with a date, in the shape of a habit: when this happens, instead of what I usually do, I will. Then the coach asks what could get in the way, and receives the answer as true. A named obstacle may be a real one, and it is allowed to change or end the test.

The read of the test opens the next session, inside the rightsizing and never ahead of what the person arrived with. Two questions: what happened, and what are you seeing now. The person says what it meant. An unmade choice is data, not failure, and the person is never made to feel small for one; what stopped them is now in the room and the person decides what to make of it. When a test confirms the belief, the work goes back to the direction rather than into problem solving: given this is true, who do you want to be with it, and what is the choice now.

## 5. The Method: Entry, the Loop, the Water's Edge, and Exit

### Entry

One conversation, or two, before the loop begins. The coach helps visibly from the first minutes rather than selling. The engagement is agreed in writing: roles, what each person wants, confidentiality and how it is kept, the time period, and the boundary of the work. The written agreement says that the coach is a thinking partner and not an adviser, and that between sessions the person will be choosing and reflecting. It is read before the first session and never spoken as an opener.

The direction comes before the problem. The coach asks who the person wants to be and what they want their life to look like in ten to fifteen years, far enough out to get past rehearsed answers, and listens for the answer that lights something rather than the one that sounds responsible. A person who declines the question, or cannot safely dream, is asked instead how they want to be acting and seen this month, and that is a full answer. The direction is written in the person's words and the presenting problem is placed inside it. This is the arc every later walk is measured against.

The scoreboard is one line inside the direction: what the person will be choosing differently, in their words, and one named person who would notice. It measures the person's choices, never other people's behavior, because other people are not the one in the chair.

### The Loop

Every session runs the same shape.

Rightsize. Contract the session in the person's words. Offer the read of the last micro choice inside it.

Walk. Hear the story and replay it. Ask for the example. Ask what they did. Walk the decisions until the person names the choice point. When the person has stayed on other people for a session or more, one question turns it back: what is the real challenge here for you. Before going to another level, ask, shall we go deeper here, and then silence. When heavy material arrives, the coach receives it, does not follow it backward, and hands the choice back: that is yours to take further or not, and what do we need to do right now.

Read. At the choice point, the constraint read: want, fuel, hands, fear. Say which binds, in the person's words, and say plainly when it is not a belief.

Name. Help the person say the belief as a sentence they can look at. Ask what would have to happen for them to find out whether it is true.

Choose. Design the micro choice together, with its two honesty questions, a date, and what could get in the way.

Close. What are you seeing now that you were not seeing an hour ago. What will you choose, and by when. What was most useful for you. The insight is spoken because an insight that stays in the head leaks out before the week is over.

The loop is not a script. Most sessions spend most of their time in the walk. Some never reach a name, and that is right when the person is not ready. The loop exists so the coach always knows where the conversation is and what the next honest step is.

### The Water's Edge

The coach brings the person to water and does not need them to drink. But a coach who keeps walking a person to the same edge without saying so is not being kind; they are being paid to be quiet. So the method names the moment when the walk has been done, the read says fear, a belief has been named, and two micro choices in a row have gone unmade. It is named once, without heat, as a replay rather than a verdict: here is what you said you want, in your words; here is the choice you named; here is what has happened twice. Then the question is handed back open: what do you want to do with that, and what is the work we really need to be doing here.

Whatever comes back is honored. The person may choose a smaller choice, and the loop resumes. The person may name something more true, that the outcome has changed, that something else is more pressing, or that what they want is to keep what they have rather than change it, and the engagement rightsizes to that, which is a real answer and not a failure. The person may say, in whatever words, that they do not want to make this choice. Then the coach says plainly that coaching cannot move the outcome without one, respects the answer without a lecture, and the engagement pauses or closes cleanly with the door left open: I will bring you back here whenever you want to come. The coach does not run the loop a third time against that answer and does not take it personally. A coach who notices frustration in themselves on the day the edge is due postpones the naming to the next session, because an edge named with heat is a push, and a pushed person resists, complies, or checks out.

### Exit

The engagement ends when the person can run the loop on themselves: hear their own story, walk their decisions to the choice point, read what binds, name the belief, and design the micro choice without the coach. The last sessions make this explicit. The person brings a new problem and runs the walk while the coach keeps company and says almost nothing. The scoreboard is read by the person at the final session.

## 6. The Executive Interface

The people this method is for run companies and are good at reading a room. They decide in the first minutes whether the coach is worth their time, and they decide it on two things at once: is this person credible enough that I am not wasting an hour, and is this person real enough that I can say the thing. A coach who manages only the first is a consultant with questions. A coach who manages only the second is a kind person who gets steamrolled. The interface is both, and it is held at the same time.

Credibility is earned by not wasting them. The direction conversation is done briskly and well. The rightsizing is in their words and takes two minutes. The walk does not interrogate the backstory they already know. The constraint read says plainly when the block is not a belief, and the advice line says plainly when the coach is stepping out to tell them something. An executive trusts a coach who can say I do not know and I will not pretend this is coaching, because they are surrounded by people who cannot.

Vulnerability is going first. The person with more standing sets how deep the room can go by what they reveal, and in a coaching session the coach goes first with something small and true: a reaction named as a reaction, a wondering offered as a wondering, a plain I am not sure where this goes. The tell is a perfect transcript with a clunky recording: a coach who never misses a word and is not in the room. The human in the chair says what they see with a question mark and lets it be wrong. Executives know the difference in the first minute, and they only tell the truth to the second one.

Equal enough. Humility with a high status person has a floor. A coach who shrinks reads as weak, and a weak coach is not trusted with the thing. The coach declines handed over power, refuses flattery inwardly, and does not perform deference, and when the room needs it the coach is willing to be bigger: to hold a silence the person wants filled, to ask the question that changes the subject back, to name the water's edge. Equal enough means neither above nor beneath, and it is read from how the coach sits in the chair before a word is spoken.

Not in their way. The guiding question of every session is what is the least I need to do today for this person to feel seen and heard and have new insight into their own stuff. Executives experience over helping as being managed, and they experience under presence as absence. The coach is fully there and does almost nothing, and the person does the walk. The test of every intervention: who will gain an insight from this. If it is the coach, it is not asked.

## 7. The Human Behind the Method

The coach's state sets the emotional tone of the room, because the state of the person with more standing is contagious and in a coaching session that is the coach. Fear, impatience, and judgment in the coach are felt before anything is said. They are the difference between a person who trusts the confidentiality and a person who feels safe enough to say the thing.

Before every session: am I in a good place to have this conversation. If not, postpone, and say why in one sentence. The coach arrives with empty hands and no plan B, because a plan manages the coach's anxiety and not the person's need.

When the coach notices a reaction in themselves, and before any challenge, the inner check runs: what did I observe, what am I feeling about it, what have I already decided, and am I about to ask something I already think I know the answer to. The coach watches for their own tells: filling a silence, explaining a question, stacking two questions, reaching for a technique, telling a story of their own, and asking a question that changes the subject when the conversation gets uncomfortable. That last one is the quiet failure. It feels like a question and it is an escape, and the person pays for it. When the coach catches themselves steering, the repair is to say so in one sentence, I notice I am trying to get you somewhere and I am sorry, and hand the conversation back; a steer that is owned builds trust and a steer that is hidden spends it.

The coach watches for one more tell, the one this method is most exposed to: frustration with a person who will not drink. It shows up as a sharper question, a shorter silence, a walk that turns into an audit, and an edge named a session too early. The remedy is the stance, re-read as often as it takes: the coach loves the walk and does not need the drink. A coach who cannot find that on a given day postpones.

The coach releases judgment by admitting they are judgmental, catching it as a sensation in the body, exhaling, and returning. The coach receives rather than listens for data: words, tone, and what the body is doing, taken in as valid, with empathy that senses what the person feels without feeling it with them. The coach does not ask did you make progress today, because that question is really was I any good today. The coach's own history belongs in supervision, and sometimes in therapy, not in the room.

## 8. The Two Lines

The advice line. Inside the loop there is no hybrid coaching. The coach does not slip advice in with a question mark attached, does not ask have you thought of, and does not ask why, which is diagnosis dressed as curiosity. When the person says I do not know, the coach first tests whether they know and are afraid, because that is what I do not know usually means. When the constraint read says hands, the method does not pretend otherwise. The coach asks permission to step out of coaching, says plainly that this is now advice, gives it short, as an idea and never a prescription, and steps back in by asking what the person thinks of it. A person cannot be coached out of nothing, and a coach who hides advice inside coaching teaches the person to wait for the easy way out.

The therapy line. The method works on present choices and the beliefs under them, tested in future action. It does not excavate history, does not ask the person to account for where a belief came from, and reads who the person takes themselves to be only from what they say now. A person's history is where their deepest beliefs were built, and it is also where coaching drifts into work it is not contracted or trained for. A coach does not need the person's backstory or their secret for the person to change. When what is in the room is trauma, a pattern that needs a therapist, or distress that exceeds the work, the coach says so kindly, keeps the relationship, and refers.

## 9. Delivery

The client is the person being coached, who contracts personally even when an organization pays. A sponsoring organization may set the context and fund the work; it does not set the agenda, receive the content, or receive anything beyond what the person agrees in writing at Entry.

Delivery is one coach, one person. Engagements run [six months] by default, with sessions [every two weeks], so that every micro choice has time to be made and every read lands while it is fresh. Shorter engagements are offered for a single presenting problem and are still run as the loop.

Three months after exit, one short conversation: the person reads the scoreboard again and names what held and what reverted. Only that read appears in any claim the practice makes, because the change the method cares about shows up between sessions and after them, not in the room.

The practice keeps the coach honest the way the method keeps the person honest. Sessions are recorded with the person's consent and reviewed. Mentor coaching works from recordings, because self report is not enough. Supervision is the standing home for what happens inside the coach when things go wrong. Renewal is daily and small, because a coach in a closed state cannot open anyone. The practice keeps a named referral path for the therapy line rather than improvising one in the moment.

## 10. The Standing Disciplines

These are the rules the method imposes on itself. The coach may not grant themselves exceptions.

Love the walk, not the drink. The coach's worth never rides on whether the person changes, and a coach who cannot find that on the day postpones.

Direction before problem. The person's chosen future is on the table before the gap, and the presenting problem is held inside it.

The replay before the question, and the silence after it belongs to the person.

The person walks their own decisions and names the choice point before the coach does.

The constraint read before any culprit is named, and present but not binding is a sanctioned finding.

The person names the belief in their own words, and the person's wording stands.

Every micro choice carries its two honesty questions, and the person's named obstacle is received as true.

Every session closes with what the person is seeing now and what they will choose by when, and an unmade choice is data, never failure.

The edge is named once, after two unmade choices, as a replay and never a verdict, and every answer to it is honored, including no.

No hybrid coaching, and the therapy line held: present choices, future action, no excavation, and a referral when the room needs one.

The coach never scores the engagement. The person reads the scoreboard, at the end and at three months.
