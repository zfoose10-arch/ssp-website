# Summit Spring Partners — Brand Rules

Version 2. July 2026. Replaces the prior mountain-blue and slate palette entirely.

## Palette

Six colors. No others. No tints, no shades, no derived ramps, no generated variants.

- `#1B2C3E` **background.** Every page, everywhere. The base the entire brand sits on.
- `#DEDEDC` **primary text.** Headlines and body.
- `#BCBCB9` **secondary text.** Captions, muted detail.
- `#5E6975` **structure.** Borders, dividers, rules. Never text; contrast against the background is insufficient.
- `#CDC1B3` **accent, light warm.** The italic summary line that closes a section, link hover states, the active nav item.
- `#908472` **accent, deep warm.** Eyebrow labels and small uppercase section labels only. Never a sentence, never body text.

Printed documents, ruled by Zane 2026-09-24 for the Word and Excel templates of Flo's document lane: the page is white paper, headings are `#1B2C3E`, body text is `#5E6975` and rules are `#BCBCB9`, which read well against paper, and eyebrow labels stay `#908472`. Slides follow the Decks section below.

The Word header, ruled by Zane 2026-09-25 for every SSP Word template: the logo mark at 2.5 times its first height, right aligned in the header block, moved from the left by his second ruling of that day; the SUMMIT SPRING PARTNERS wordmark centered in the header both horizontally and vertically; the header band grown to fit.

## The warm rule

The two warm values are the only warmth in an otherwise cool system, and that is why they work. They keep that power only if they stay rare.

Together, the warms cover a small fraction of any screen. They never appear adjacent to each other. Each has one job and does not take the other's.

`#CDC1B3` is bright enough to carry emphasis. It is the color of the italic line that closes a section.

`#908472` is deliberately quiet. It sits above a headline as an eyebrow label and recedes beneath it. It must never carry a sentence.

If a page begins to feel warm or tan, the correction is more whitespace, not less accent.

## No filled surfaces

There is no elevated surface color in this system. Where a card or filled block would normally be used, use a thin `#5E6975` rule instead.

The site is prose. It does not need containers.

## Logo

**Logo files are never modified.** Not cropped, not recolored, not redrawn, not partially erased. If a logo does not work in a given context, the layout changes, not the logo.

The mark is a mountain with a spring flowing out of it. The spring is the central image of the brand and must always render intact.

- Mark only: nav, at small scale.
- Full lockup: footer.

## Decks

Ruled by Zane 2026-10-20 at the deck template mint: the presentation template spec of that day, as amended by his picks of that day, governs slides, the PowerPoint template SSP PowerPoint.pptx and every deck built from it, and where a line of this section and the spec differ, this section holds. - **Palette.** The spec's six: `#222737` deep navy, `#474A58` dark slate, `#D3D4D9` light cool gray, `#8B7E74` warm taupe as the one scarce accent, `#6F727C` medium slate, `#ADACAE` light neutral gray. No second accent.

- **Three exceptions to the rules above, for slides only.** The generated contour texture on the title, a light gray panel behind the one screen mockup, and the mockup's soft shadow baked into its picture.

## Typography

- **Headlines:** Source Serif 4. Editorial, confident, with spine.
- **Body:** Inter. Neutral, disciplined, legible at small sizes on dark backgrounds.
- **Nav, section labels, eyebrow text:** Inter, uppercase, generously letterspaced. Echoes the spaced capitals in the wordmark.

Body text sets in a comfortable reading measure, roughly 65 to 75 characters. Long-form prose is never stretched to the full width of the viewport.

Headlines can be large. The copy is short and declarative and can carry the weight.

## Whitespace

Vertical space between sections should be roughly twice what feels necessary. This is the single most important rule in the system.

The audience is a CEO deciding whether the founder is a serious practitioner. Crowded type reads as cheap. Air reads as expensive. When in doubt, add space.

## Feel

Sharp, not soft. Editorial, not decorative. Still, not busy.

The reference points are the financial press and top tier advisory firms. The anti-reference is anything that looks like a startup or a SaaS marketing site.

## Never do this

- No gradients, except the hero scrim (2.1).
- No glows.
- No drop shadows.
- No decorative geometry, no abstract polygons, no floating shapes.
- No rounded, friendly, geometric sans typefaces.
- No stock photography, except the one hero photograph (2.1).
- No icons used as decoration. If an icon does not carry meaning, remove it.
- No color outside the six above and the deep ground of 2.1.
- No animation beyond the quietest possible transition.
- No filled cards or containers.
- No modification of the logo.

## Posture

The site is a credibility resource, not a conversion funnel. It should look like it comes from someone who is not in a hurry and does not need to impress you.

Restraint is the entire point. When a decision is close, choose the quieter option.
