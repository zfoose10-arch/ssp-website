## 16. Consulting, Phase 2, written contract paragraph

Before: Each move is anchored to a written contract: the change, its owner, its leading indicators, its definition of done. Structured check-ins track whether it is holding, fragile, regressing, or stalled, and failure signals are named immediately. The engagement closes when the new pattern runs in standing operating cadence without external presence.

After: Each move is written down before it starts: the change, who owns it, one thing you will do differently as CEO to back it, and what would tell us to expand it or stop it. Check-ins read each move as holding, fragile, regressing, or stalled, and no move is judged before it has had time to work.

Why: Every probe carries a chief executive commitment and expand or stop criteria, and no verdict is given inside a probe's delay (Signal Integrity 5 Movement 3).

## 26. Coaching, What an engagement looks like

Before: Coaching runs in defined arcs, not open-ended relationships. A typical engagement is six months, meeting weekly, with a focus named at the start and revisited at the midpoint. Between sessions the work continues, because the patterns under examination only show themselves under live pressure. At the end of the arc, we decide together whether the work is done or whether a new focus has earned another one.

After: Coaching runs in defined arcs, not open-ended relationships. A typical engagement is six months. It starts with where you want to go, in your own words, and one line on what you will be choosing differently. Between sessions the work continues, because the patterns under examination only show themselves under live pressure. The arc ends when you can do this work on your own, and three months later we read that line again together.

Why: Weekly sessions contradict the method's default cadence, and the method sets the direction and its one line scoreboard at entry, exits when the person can run the loop alone, and reads the line at three months (Plain Sight 5 Entry, 5 Exit, 9).
