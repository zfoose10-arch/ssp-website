# Writing rules for the site's copy

Zane's standing rules, in force for every rendered string and every line of the copy doc.

- No em dashes, ever. No hyphen used as punctuation to join clauses in place of a comma, colon or semicolon; restructure the sentence instead. Hyphens that are part of a word's spelling are fine (well-known, mother-in-law).
- Plain and warm beats clever. Down to earth, professional, grounded in confidence and grace. A clever aphorism headline was rejected once as gimmicky and posturing; the plainer line won.
- One qualifier in claims about the reader ("the problem isn't usually the problem"), no qualifiers in claims about Zane himself. Leave room for the reader's own conviction without hedging into unsureness.
- Outcome claims are hedged and ranged, never promised.
- Second person to the reader where the copy speaks to them; first person for Zane.
- Short sentences, the reader's own nouns, no jargon. A method's instrument or movement is described by what it does for the reader, not named as a term, unless the name is itself plain English.
- Forward looking language only on the AI band; nothing about why a first attempt stalled.
- Nothing that names another client, a figure not yet true, or a proof not yet earned.
